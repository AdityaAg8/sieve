# Limitations

## What Sieve does not do

Sieve is a triage layer, not a scanner. It writes no detection rules and no signatures. It
decides which of *another* tool’s findings deserve attention, and it can only be as complete as
what it was given. A tool nobody flagged and nobody handed to Sieve is a tool Sieve never saw.

Sieve reads static definitions. It says nothing about what a server does at runtime, what it
sends over the wire, or what a tool does when called.

## Where it is weakest

- **Poisoned training samples are synthetic.** Real poisoned tools in the wild are rare, so the
  poisoned class is largely generated, with a hand-authored fraction and a small real slice held
  back for evaluation only. Numbers on the real slice are the ones to trust.
- **The judge is an LLM.** It can be argued with by the text it is reading. The self-defence
  suite is two-sided — it must not be talked out of a finding, and it must not be talked into one
  — and the model card reports the result rather than claiming general injection resistance.
- **The benign/imperative-benign boundary is genuinely hard.** Two careful people do not always
  agree on it, which is why agreement is published three ways instead of once.

## What would change this

Real poisoned tools. The corpus grows as they are reported, and every number here is regenerated
from committed predictions when it does, with the holdout opened once per published version and
the log published beside the result.
